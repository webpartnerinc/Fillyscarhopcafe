Our Story

A Main Street Drive-In,
Reborn as Filly’s

Same corner of Main Street, same griddle sizzle, same window trays — a classic Utah drive-in carrying seven decades of carhop tradition into today.

How We Got Here

From Cluff’s Drive-Inn to Filly’s

Long before the Filly’s sign went up, this little drive-in on Fillmore’s Main Street was already a legend. Cars rolled in for burgers, fries and thick shakes while carhops clipped trays to doors and families made it a Friday-night ritual.

For decades the place was known as Cluff’s — a true family operation. The Cluff family kept the grill hot and the tradition alive through three generations, with the recipes and the rhythm of the place passed down like heirlooms.

When the Cluffs finally decided to retire, the community held its breath. Then a local Fillmore family stepped in, took over the same location with the same great staff, and in 2025 hung a new name above the door: Filly’s Carhop Cafe.

New name. Same fryers, same shakes, same warm welcome — and a fresh chapter for a Utah classic.

A Little History Lesson

So… What’s a Carhop?

Before drive-thrus, there were carhops — and Filly’s still does it the original way.

Back in the 1950s, a new kind of restaurant swept America: the drive-in. You parked under the lights, and a carhop — often on skates in the early days — came out to take your order at the car window.

When the food was ready, it came back out on a metal tray that hooked right over your rolled-down window. Burgers, fries and shakes, eaten without ever leaving your seat, with the radio on and the whole family along for the ride.

Most drive-ins traded their trays for speaker boxes and drive-thru lanes decades ago. At Filly’s, the old ritual survives: roll in, park, and let a real live carhop bring dinner to your door. It’s slow food, served fast — with a side of nostalgia.
